## Control contract

At active tick `k`, current geometry and Jacobians use `q[k], v[k]`. The most recently completed native interval is `(k-1) -> k`; forces and contact positions remain owned by its prestate. The recorder copies every owned interval contact, not only its largest load. The adapter reconstructs the prior transform from saved `q[k-1]`, converts each contact position to a material coordinate on its actual body, transports that point to `q[k]`, and computes both coherent prior velocity and current velocity. A load is never assigned to the newly selected minimum vertex. Nonfoot contact is retained with `leg=-1` and cannot supply support.

For each of the 30 meshes, every compiled vertex participates in minimum selection. Five separate minimum-point rows per swinging leg address all-foot clearance. Tarsus5 centroid is the transformed arithmetic mean of that mesh's compiled vertices. Forces retain native units; position and translational Jacobians use millimetres and millimetres/radian. All active maps are 42 distinct unit-gear hinges, and root/passive coordinates remain observations. The frozen plane is world `z=0`, with gravity along negative world z.

The intent oscillator retains the source cadence/asymmetry mapping and Euler update. Realized phase is separately named and stored. It catches up at no more than twice the current intent increment, stops at each segment boundary, and fails at lag greater than π/2 or a gate wait greater than 500 active ticks. Arrival at a boundary spends no leftover phase in the following segment. The next active tick may change the mode at that boundary, without advancing through a second segment in the same tick.

The selected swing interval is split into equal LIFT, TRANSFER and LAND thirds. LIFT holds AP until 30 coherent intervals are unloaded with whole-foot minimum strictly above .02 mm. TRANSFER commands monotone cubic AP with the native two-endpoint excursion, while all five mesh normal tasks retain .05 mm height. LAND anchors the actual distal world XY, limits downward speed, damps every loaded proximal contact tangent, and enables adhesion. Thirty distal-positive intervals are a controller transition, not a replacement for original all-foot acceptance. SUPPORT loss enters REACQUIRE at unchanged phase and blocks new releases.

The initial phase remains the native reset phase and its first cycle is explicitly partial. A partial TRANSFER uses its observed initial phase as that segment's local start, as specified in the selected law. The initial native magnitude is zero. Normal releases latch the current proposed intent magnitude; no excursion floor is added. No cyclic phase reset, skipped cycle or timeout is counted as success.

Release admission uses acquired, adhesion-enabled support contacts. It removes the requesting leg before testing the capture point against a hull eroded by .02 mm. Requests are processed in descending intent lag, then fixed leg order, and each granted release immediately removes its contacts before the next request. Positive nonfoot force, pending LAND/REACQUIRE, or `up_z <= .8` vetoes new releases. This geometric and inertial heuristic is not a support-wrench certificate and does not use adhesive tensile reserve.

The shared planar body twist fits the native stance joint-velocity intent over all currently SUPPORT contacts. The body height reference is the first active thorax height; the .05 s body feedback corrects height/roll/pitch. It does not specify an arena XY goal or yaw target. Each leg solves the selected regularized 7×7 problem, then integrates correction relative to the native angle reference. Correction increments have norm at most `800*dt*C_i`; the correction ball has radius `80*C_i`; the final command intersects native joint/control intervals. The old phase-dependent retraction/stumbling angle offsets are replaced. Gain 45, force range ±65, adhesion gain 40 and body geometry remain fixed.

## Transaction and checkpoint contract

`Controller.propose()` validates the complete observation and calculates all six legs in private state. `Controller.commit()` and `NativePort.apply()` share one complete check of a process-local controller owner capability, monotone ephemeral revision, exact predecessor checkpoint digest, scientific generation, complete successor state and action. Model binding is not owner identity. Successful commits and restores advance the ephemeral revision; even same-state and ABA restores invalidate all outstanding proposals. Owner/revision remain outside the scientific checkpoint, so legitimate checkpoint continuation remains identical. A late-leg invalid reference or failed solve leaves the previous state/action intact. `NativePort.apply()` additionally checks that authoritative `qpos`, `qvel` and tick have not changed since proposal, stages the complete 48-entry native control vector, and commits control/controller/sensor state together with rollback on an assignment exception. The control vector uses the exact native anatomical-to-output map; this is the same unit-transmission mapping used by `apply_locomotion_action`.

For `c <= 1e-4`, drive validation precedes every observation/provider access, and all controller/sensor/diagnostic/RNG/angle/adhesion state is held exactly. The scientific runner may continue recording the unchanged physics under held control, as the existing stop protocol does. Its interval recorder is separate from controller diagnostics; it records completed silent intervals so the next active action has the immediately preceding physical prestate. Consecutive active observations require exact equality of prior qpos/qvel with the last accepted endpoint before intent/reference access. A gap in active observations resets dwell histories because unseen intervals cannot supply a controller dwell. Across that intentional silence gap, the immediately preceding physical state is established by the separate recorder; it is not compared with an obsolete sensor endpoint. The pure port does not advance physical time or update body drives; those are the runner's existing per-step responsibilities.

Pure controller checkpoints serialize the full versioned state, native configuration and RNG, realized phase/mode/cycle state, counters, release/landing trajectory state, previous native/applied commands, actual corrections, prior observation and nested diagnostics. Exact float and unsigned RNG bytes round-trip through JSON. Restore validates a private decoded state before replacing anything. Uninitialized state requires its exact empty-history/reset structure. Initialized state requires the saved observation, its exact last_tick and interval IDs, valid generation, native/release magnitudes in [0,1.8], nonnegative excursion/load totals, coherent force/dwell and mode/action relationships, native drive configuration, complete per-leg diagnostics, and the native RandomState MT19937 tuple (624 uint32 words, bounded position and Gaussian cache fields). No RNG operation is needed for schema validation.

Full native checkpoints additionally use `mj_copyData`, the integration state, numeric native cache digests, body tick/drives, sensor observation, recorder state including the complete latest interval, and immutable registration/binding identity. Restore creates and validates a detached staged MjData before touching the authoritative body, and retains a full rollback copy. `record_pre_step()` copies actual authoritative qpos/qvel/tick before the caller steps physics. `record_completed()` rejects a material packet unless its prestate exactly matches that independent saved copy; it changes only recorder state. Active prepare and apply require that owned packet, and compare its contact payload with the observation. `interval_state()/interval_from_state()` provide serialization. A pending pre-step capture at positive tick is deliberately not a complete checkpoint; complete it after the sole caller-owned physical step. A checkpoint at physical tick >=1 without that packet and its saved prestate is rejected as incomplete. The pure `restored_payload()` checks controller/sensor/recorder schemas, input drives, control action and physical clock before native cache work. At the action boundary, body tick equals the last observation tick and the endpoint/material packet must match. One completed step later, the recorder prestate must equal that observation endpoint; the new body endpoint may differ. Longer silence gaps retain older controller/sensor state with the current coherent recorder packet. Initial controller state and physical tick zero are also explicit valid formats. Native restore subsequently verifies full cache and integration payloads in detached data before atomic assignment. These native paths are source implementations only; their physical bitwise continuation is unrun and must be independently reviewed and later tested.
